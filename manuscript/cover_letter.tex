\textbf{Subject: Submission of the manuscript ``Two-Time-Scale Coordination of Vehicle-to-Grid Voltage Support and Frequency Reserve in a Two-Area System Using Proximal Policy Optimization''}

\vspace{6pt}
Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit the above manuscript for consideration in
\emph{IEEE Access}. The work is a complete rebuild of a manuscript that
was previously reviewed by the journal and rejected under the title
``Coordinated V2G Voltage and Frequency-State Regulation in a Two-Area
System: A Gaussian Actor--Critic Simulation Framework'' (manuscript ID:
\textcolor{red}{[previous ID]}). The reviewers of that version found,
correctly, that the controller had not been trained and that the
network and frequency models were algebraic surrogates. Rather than
revise the text, we rebuilt the study from the model upward and are
submitting it as a new manuscript. A point-by-point response to the
earlier reviews is enclosed so that the Editor can see how each comment
was addressed.

\textbf{What the paper does.} An aggregated fleet of vehicle-to-grid
electric vehicles can support distribution voltages and provide
frequency reserve, but both services draw on the same converter rating
and stored energy. The paper formulates their coordination as a
two-time-scale control problem: every hour each area aggregator chooses
a fleet power set-point and the share of its plugged-in converter rating
held as frequency reserve. The set-points are evaluated by AC power flow
on IEEE 33-bus feeders in two areas with an energy-consistent fleet
model; the reserve sets the gain and limit of a V2G droop loop in a
dynamic two-area load-frequency control model with inertia, governors,
a tie line, and automatic generation control, simulated at a 20-ms
step. A proximal policy optimization agent is trained with \NSeed{}
independent seeds and compared, under identical conditions, with
uncontrolled charging, a rule-based volt-watt and droop controller, and
a day-ahead optimization with perfect foresight.

\textbf{Main findings.} On 30 held-out days the learned policy lowers
the daily performance cost by \TRedCostDAO\%, \TRedCostRB\%, and
\TRedCostUC\% relative to the three benchmarks, reduces undervoltage
by \TRedUvPuHDAO\% and the mean frequency nadir by
\TRedNadirMeanHzDAO\% relative to the day-ahead optimization, and keeps
its advantage when that benchmark is re-optimized for every reserve
share and even against a per-day oracle choice of the share. The paper
also reports what the method does not achieve: some hours of the weak
residential feeder stay below 0.95~p.u.\ with every controller, a
downward activation of the committed reserve can push further hours
below the band, and consecutive-day operation shows a downward drift of
the residential fleet energy that a soft terminal penalty does not
prevent. These limits are quantified in the manuscript rather than left
to the reader.

\textbf{Why the work suits \emph{IEEE Access}.} The paper combines
power-system modeling (verified AC power flow and a dynamic LFC model
in physical units), reinforcement learning with a stated training and
evaluation protocol, and an unusually complete reproducibility package.
The energy exchanged by the droop loop is included in the fleet energy
balance and checked against exact 3600-s simulations; every number in
the manuscript is recomputed from the raw result files by an
independent verification script (84 checks); and the code, trained
policies, result files, checksums, and exact package versions are
released at \url{https://github.com/kenoto99/v2g-ppo-two-area}
(archived on Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23851585).

\textbf{Declarations.} The manuscript is original, has not been
published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have
read and approved the submitted version and agree to its submission to
\emph{IEEE Access}. The author list differs from the previously
